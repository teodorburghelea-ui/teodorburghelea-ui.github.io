Teodor (Teo) Burghelea

Directeur de Recherche CNRS · Laboratoire de Thermique et Énergie de Nantes (LTeN, UMR 6607), Nantes Université

Flow reshapes the microstructure of a complex fluid, and the microstructure reshapes the flow. I study that loop as a problem in nonlinear dynamics and critical phenomena.

I am an experimental physicist working on polymer solutions, yield-stress gels, polymer melts and suspensions. I treat their flow transitions (yielding, elastic turbulence, instabilities without inertia) as one class of problems, measure them locally, and use what we learn to control mixing, heat transfer and emulsification.

The common thread

Transitions, not thresholds

Textbook models give complex fluids sharp switches: a yield stress, a critical Weissenberg number, a stability limit. In experiments, these turn out to be transitions. They have coexisting phases, hysteresis, critical slowing down and imperfect bifurcations.

So I borrow the tools built for phase transitions and chaos: Landau theory, Ising-type statistical models, Lagrangian chaos and Batchelor-regime scalar turbulence. I test them against experiments that resolve the flow in space and time. The same approach works for a hair gel, a polymer solution and a molten polymer.

Five ideas

What the work changed

Each line of research started from an accepted picture and ended with a different one.

01 · Elastic turbulence

Turbulence without inertia

Accepted picture
At vanishing Reynolds number, flows are laminar and microchannels cannot mix.
What we showed
A trace of polymer makes microflows chaotic. They mix in the Batchelor regime, carry heat up to four times better, and become turbulent without hysteresis.
PRL 2004, 2006 · PRE 2004 · JNNFM 2015 · Phys. Fluids 2017 · PRF 2026 02 · Yielding

The yield stress is a transition, not a number

Accepted picture
Carbopol is a “model” Herschel–Bulkley fluid that switches sharply from solid to liquid.
What we showed
Yielding is gradual, hysteretic and mediated by elasticity, through solid–fluid coexistence. An Ising-type model predicts a Curie-like threshold for irreversibility.
Rheol. Acta 2009 · Soft Matter 2015, 2017, 2024 · JNNFM 2020, 2021 03 · Stability

Instabilities without inertia

Accepted picture
Slow, viscous flows are stable, and a yield stress suppresses convection.
What we showed
Strong stratification destabilises inertia-free flows. It can be stratification of viscosity, elastic stress, crystals or temperature. One example is chaos in a sheared melting pot; another is convection in yield-stress gels.
EPL 2002 · Phys. Fluids 2007 · JNNFM 2011, 2014 · J. Fluid Mech. 2021 04 · Measurement

What you see is what you get

Accepted picture
The extensional viscosity overshoot of LDPE is a molecular property.
What we showed
It appears exactly when secondary necks form, so it is a macroscopic instability. Local imaging, rather than integral signals, decides what rheometry can claim.
J. Rheol. 2009 · JNNFM 2010, 2011, 2015 · PRF 2020 05 · Transport

Mixing what does not want to mix

Accepted picture
Laminar chaotic advection mixes any viscous fluid if you stir it the right way.
What we showed
Above Bn ≈ 5 a yield stress makes mixing fail catastrophically, because unyielded blobs block the scalar cascade. Shear thinning helps instead, and polymer additives enable drag-reduction-assisted emulsification.
PRF 2021, 2023 · IJHMT 2022 · TCFD 2023 · JNNFM 2024

How we work

Four habits

Lens

Transitions as critical phenomena

Bifurcation theory and the physics of phase transitions make yielding, instabilities and the onset of chaos comparable across materials.

Measurement

Local before integral

Velocity, deformation and microstructure fields resolved in space and time (DPIV, micro-PIV, fluorescent labelling) come before any macroscopic number.

Instruments

Built in-house

Rheo-optical accessories, micromilled channels, an extensional rheometer with visualisation, and our own DPIV and image-analysis codes.

Theory

Minimal models, then simulation

Microstructural, Gibbs-field and reduced dynamical models, alongside Gerris/Basilisk and tensorial elasto-viscoplastic simulations, with applied mathematicians abroad.

What comes next

Open questions we are working on

  • Closing the loop on yielding

    Feed microstructure statistics measured in situ into the statistical models, and test their predictions in real, non-rheometric flows.

  • Is elasto-viscoplastic yielding universal?

    Carbopol, dense micro-algae and entangled xanthan all yield through hysteresis. What is shared, and what is the order parameter?

  • Elastic turbulence under confinement

    Measured velocity-gradient fluctuations exceed theory by about 20×. Boundary layers and walls are the suspects.

  • Design rules for mixing structured fluids

    From rotating arc-wall mixers to microfluidic emulsification and gentle processing of micro-algae.
